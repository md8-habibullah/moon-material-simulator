# S013 source issues: NASA NTRS 20030020808, "Predictions for Radiation Shielding Materials" (Kiefer, W&M, NCC-1-327)

General: the PDF is a scan (Acrobat Capture OCR). The text layer garbles most table and figure numbers (e.g. Table 1 "3.18 x" with the exponent lost, "i.7ox 10-5" for 1.70 x 10-5), so the auditor should check values against the rendered page image, not the text layer.

- p.5 text vs p.9 Table 1 | unit inconsistency | "modest reduction in cell death rate at thicknesses of 10 mg/cm2 and greater" vs "Thickness (g cm-2)" | Text says mg/cm2 but the table (and Fig. 1, "x, g/cm2") use g/cm2. The table is consistent with g/cm2.
- p.5 text vs p.12 Fig. 2 | beam energy mismatch | "The beam energy of the BNL 56Fe experiment is 1.05 A GeV" vs "1.06 GeV/amu 56Fe" (Fig. 2 title and caption) | 1.05 vs 1.06 GeV per nucleon for the same experiment.
- p.5 text vs p.12 Fig. 2 | text contradicts figure legend | "the best target for dose reduction was an epoxy, with poIymer/regolith composite third" vs legend "MarsRegolith" | The third-best bar (3.44E-02) is labelled "MarsRegolith". No polymer/regolith composite appears in the legend, so it is unclear whether this target is plain Martian regolith or a composite. Binder and ratio are unknown.
- p.12 Fig. 2 | undefined sample labels | "CH2*", "Al-CH2-7", "Al-CH2-10", "CH2/Li6", "CH2/LiC", "CH2+B", "Gr-Epox5", "Gr-Epox10", "B-Epoxy" | Target compositions, thicknesses and the meaning of the asterisk are never defined. Only "CH2 is polyethylene" is given. The figure is labelled "PRELIMINARY".
- p.5 text vs p.13 Fig. 3 | fluence in text does not match where the figure shows the difference | "specimen 101 with 40% LaRC-SI by mass is much better than specimen 102 with 20% LaRC-SI for shielding as the fluence of the beam reaches 4 x 10^8 /cm2" vs Fig. 3 points | At about 4E8 p/cm2 the two specimens are close (about 17 vs 23 errors). The large difference is at about 4E9 p/cm2 (about 59 vs 353). The text probably means 4 x 10^9. Fig. 3 also has no specimen-101 marker at 1E9, where 102 has about 71.
- p.6 text vs p.10 Table 2 / p.7 Conclusions | conclusion overstates the data | "Composites of LaRC-SI/regolith and polyethylene/regolith are shown to have good structural properties as well as being effective shields from GCR radiation" vs Table 2 "Specimen 101 580 / Specimen 102 633" vs "No Shield 474" | In the only neutron SEU test, both LaRC-SI/regolith specimens gave more upsets than no shield (the text says "within statistical error"). PE/regolith composites were never radiation-tested in this report, and GCR shielding of the composites was only calculated (Fig. 1), with Martian regolith.
- p.6 text vs p.14 Fig. 5(b) | text not supported by figure | "The Young's modulus of neutron-exposed specimens for both regolith 101 and 102 were decreased by 10% from the baseline" vs Fig. 5(b) | Digitized points: 101 baseline about 5.66–6.13 GPa vs neutron about 5.02–5.44 GPa (about -11%, consistent), but 102 baseline about 7.52–8.62 GPa vs neutron about 6.80–8.70 GPa (mean about -3%). This is not a clear 10% decrease for 102.
- p.6 text vs p.14 Fig. 5(b) | text not supported by figure | "the proton-exposed specimens for both regolith 101 and 102 composites experienced an increase in the Young's modulus" vs Fig. 5(b) | For 101, the proton-exposed points (about 4.98, 5.67, 7.10 GPa) are spread around the baseline (about 5.66–6.13 GPa) rather than clearly higher. For 102 the increase is visible (about 8.90–9.52 vs 7.52–8.62 GPa).
- p.14–15 Fig. 5(a)(b)(c) | specimen counts inconsistent across panels | "Baseline 101", "Proton exposed 101", ... | Visible markers per group differ between panels for the same specimens (e.g. Proton exposed 101: 3 in (a), 3 in (b), 2 in (c); Proton exposed 102: 3 in (a), 2 in (b), 3 in (c)). Markers probably overlap, so the number of specimens tested cannot be determined. No means or SDs are given.
- p.2 vs p.3 | contradictory polymer description | "LaRC-SI, a high-performance polyimide thermoset" (p.2) vs "the soluble amorphous aromatic thermoplastic polyimide, LaRC-SI" (p.3) | Thermoset vs thermoplastic for the same polymer.
- p.3 vs p.13 Fig. 4 | inconsistent thermal description | "The temperature was raised to 325 °C, which is about 10 degrees above the melting point of the polymer" vs Fig. 4 Tg about 223–229 °C and p.3 "amorphous" | An amorphous polymer has no melting point. The roughly 315 °C "melting point" is not consistent with the Tg in Fig. 4.
- p.17 Fig. 7 | missing unit | "Compressive Yield Strength" (y-axis, 0–2) | No unit is printed. Figs. 6 and 8 use ksi, and every Fig. 7 value is below the matching Fig. 6 ultimate strength, so ksi is likely.
- p.18 and p.19 | duplicated figure | "Figure 8. The compressive chord modulus of elasticity for polyethylene/regolith composites" (both pages, both printed page number 18) | Two scans of the same chart. Digitized bar heights agree within about 1 ksi after skew correction; only page 18 is extracted.
- p.18/19 Fig. 8 and p.16–17 Figs. 6–7 legend | undefined series | "Combined test rates" | Not explained (pooled average of both crosshead rates is assumed). The p.18/19 legend also misspells "test rare".
- p.3, p.6, p.16–18 | composition basis not stated for PE/regolith | "polymer/regolith compositions of 25/75, 20,80, 15/85, 10/90, 7/93, and 5/95" and "5% PE" | wt% or vol% is never stated for the polyethylene composites (for LaRC-SI it is stated "by weight"/"by mass"). The 25/75 composition is made but never reported. "20,80" is a typo for 20/80.
- p.2, p.5–6 | regolith type of the tested specimens not tied explicitly | "We have made composites of simulated lunar regolith" (p.2) vs Fig. 1 "Martian regolith/LaRC-SI composite" and Conclusions on Mars habitats | The calculations (Table 1, Fig. 1) use Martian regolith, while the made and tested specimens (101, 102, PE/regolith) use the Minnesota lunar simulant (p.2). The text for 101/102 says only "regolith". Lunar simulant is assumed in the CSV.
- p.6 text vs p.13 Fig. 4 | minor | "these composites should contain good mechanical properties up to at least 223 °C" | 223 °C matches the neat LaRC-SI Tg (100% point, about 223.4 °C). The lowest composite Tg is about 224.5 °C (70% LaRC-SI). This is consistent but rounded down.
- p.13 Fig. 4 | values only partly tied to compositions | "10% Mass Loss" curve | The 10% mass-loss curve has no points at 15% and 20% LaRC-SI, while the 5% curve does. No explanation is given. TGA atmosphere and heating rate are not stated.
- p.10 Table 2 | missing specimen details | "Polyethylene" | Thicknesses and areal densities of all Table 2 targets are not given, so the SEU counts cannot be normalised.
